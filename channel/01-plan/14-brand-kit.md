# 14 · Brand kit: avatars, banners and how to use them

Made 21 Oct 2026 (`npm run launchkit`; the pictures are drawn in `04-studio/src/Avatars.tsx`, `Banners.tsx` and `brand-art.tsx`).
Files: `03-episodes/_brand/launch-kit/avatars/` (20 PNG, 800 × 800) and `banners/` (5 PNG, 2560 × 1440, plus `-guides` copies with the safe
areas drawn). The earlier logo concepts are still in `03-episodes/_brand/out/`.

## 1. The specs they follow

| | Spec | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Avatar | 800 × 800 px, PNG or JPG, shown as a **circle** (about 98 px on the channel page, down to about 32 px in comments). Everything that matters sits in the middle circle. Ours are under 0.4 MB (sources say the limit is 2 or 4 MB). | YouTube guidance via web search: sizes agree, the file limit varies |
| Banner | 2560 × 1440 px (minimum 2048 × 1152), 16:9, PNG or JPG, 6 MB or less. Ours are under 2.3 MB. | agreed by every source I could read |
| Safe area | Text and logo stay inside **1235 × 338 px** in the middle (phones show only that). Computers show 1546 × 423, tablets 1855 × 423, TVs everything. | 1235 × 338 is what most guides quote as visible on every device (and what I remember from YouTube's page; I couldn't open it to confirm); some quote 1546 × 423, which is the computer view. We respect both |
| Thumbnail | 1280 × 720, 16:9, JPG, GIF or PNG, under 2 MB, minimum width 640 | YouTube Help, quoted by search |

## 5. My picks, and how to choose in two minutes

- **Avatar: `host-02` (شروق).** The white halo and yellow ground make the strongest shape at 32 px, in both YouTube's light and dark themes; the round
  glasses are visible even then. `host-01` is the calmer alternative. If you'd rather not show a person-shape at all: `name-03` (the open door).
- **Banner: `banner-2`** with `host-02`: the same figure in the dark banner next to the name. With a name-based avatar, use `banner-1` or `banner-4`.
- **How to choose:** open the avatar sheet, look at the smallest circle in each row (that's the comments size) and pick the one you can still
  tell apart from across the room; then open each banner's `-guides` copy and check the name and the line sit inside the red box (the phone view).

## 6. «Google-like» without breaking anyone's rules

The look is Material-like: flat shapes, big rounded forms, soft light, bright accents. It is **not** Google's: the palette is ours (blue `#3B7DF6`, not
Google's), the font is Readex Pro (open source), and there is no «G», no sparkle or star that resembles Google's AI mark, no four-colour quadrant logo,
no Google product icon and no «Google» in any name. So nothing suggests Google endorses the channel. YouTube's own rules for channel images are the
usual ones: nothing that breaks the Community Guidelines, nothing that impersonates someone else's channel or person, nothing misleading. A shadow
figure with glasses imitates nobody; if you want it to stand for you personally, say so in the About text.

## 7. Putting them on the channel

Studio → Customization → Branding → upload the picture for **Profile**, the banner for **Banner image** (YouTube lets you crop; keep the red box),
then **Publish**. Changes can take a few minutes to show everywhere. The channel's name and handle are on the same page.

## 8. Changing or adding

`npm run launchkit` draws all avatars and banners again (`avatars` or `banners` as an argument for one group). To tweak a colour, a line or the host's look,
edit `src/Avatars.tsx`, `src/Banners.tsx` or `src/brand-art.tsx`. Thumbnails: `npm run thumbs -- EP00N` with the texts in that episode's `thumbs.json`.
