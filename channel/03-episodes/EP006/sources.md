## Door 1 · Chris Gardner and the red Ferrari (early 1980s)
- Gardner met Bob Bridges, a stockbroker driving a red Ferrari, outside San Francisco General Hospital; he asked what he did
  and how he did it ("What do you do?" / "How do you do that?"); Bridges arranged meetings with branch managers at brokerage
  firms that ran training programs, including Dean Witter Reynolds; arrested the day before (unpaid parking tickets), he went
  to the interview in the clothes he was arrested in; passed the licensing exam on the first try; homeless with his young son
  for about a year; founded his own firm in 1987:
  [Encyclopedia.com](https://encyclopedia.com/education/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/gardner-chris) ·
  [Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/features/chris-gardner-the-man-who-dared-to-pursue-happiness/) ·
  [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/5443651) · Wikipedia "Chris Gardner" (check).
  Disputed and left out: whether the training paid, Bridges' income, the exact year («في بداية الثمانينات»).
  The film: *The Pursuit of Happyness* (2006) → «فيلم مشهور في 2006».
- **Evidence:** Tamir and Mitchell (Harvard), "Disclosing information about the self is intrinsically rewarding", *PNAS*
  109(21), 2012: people gave up 17% to 25% of potential earnings to talk about themselves; self-disclosure activated the
  mesolimbic dopamine (reward) system: [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3361411/) ·
  [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/enchanted-by-our-own-words/) ·
  [Fox News / WSJ figure](https://www.foxnews.com/health/neuroscience-shows-why-people-love-to-brag).

## Door 2 · [مثال توضيحي] Nouf, a maths teacher in Riyadh

## Door 3 · Dale Carnegie's dinner in New York
- From *How to Win Friends and Influence People* (1936), the chapter on becoming a good conversationalist: at a New York book
  publisher's dinner he met a famous botanist, knew nothing about botany, asked and listened all evening, and the botanist
  told the host he was "a most interesting conversationalist":
  [LitCharts](https://www.litcharts.com/lit/how-to-win-friends-and-influence-people/part-2-chapter-4) ·
  [text](https://syt-honey.github.io/How-to-Win-Friends-and-Influence-People/book/part7/chapter7-1.html) (check).
  «في فصول مسائية في نيويورك»: Carnegie taught public speaking in evening classes before the book (check).
- **Evidence:** Huang, Yeomans, Brooks, Minson and Gino (Harvard), "It Doesn't Hurt to Ask: Question-Asking Increases
  Liking", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 113(3), 430–452, 2017: 430 participants in chats; those told to ask
  at least nine questions (against at most four) were liked more; follow-up questions especially; too many or too personal
  questions can backfire: [APS](https://psychologicalscience.org/news/minds-business/asking-questions-increases-likability.html) ·
  [Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/35647952) · [paper](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/novelty/2017-huang.pdf).
  (The paper's speed-dating study is left out on purpose.)

## Door 4 · Brandon Stanton, Humans of New York
- Lost his job as a bond trader in Chicago in 2010, moved to New York to photograph people in the street, began adding their
  words under the photos, which grew into full interviews; the first book (October 2013) reached No. 1 on the New York Times
  non-fiction list: [University of Georgia](https://news.uga.edu/?p=72368) ·
  [Daily Utah Chronicle](https://dailyutahchronicle.com/?p=5652008) · Wikipedia "Brandon Stanton" (check).
  His exact question wording is not confirmed, so the script paraphrases («يسأله عن حياته… وإذا جاوب… سأله عن جوابه»).
- **Evidence:** Kardas, Kumar and Epley (University of Chicago), "Overly Shallow? Miscalibrated Expectations Create a Barrier
  to Deeper Conversation", *JPSP*, 2021/2022: 12 experiments, more than 1,800 participants; deep and shallow talks both went
  better than expected, deep ones most; people underestimated how interested partners were in learning about them:
  [APA](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2021/09/deep-conversations-strangers) ·
  [Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/deep-conversations-strangers-are-more-fulfilling-small-talk).

## Door 5 · The Wright brothers' letter
- Wilbur Wright wrote to the Smithsonian on 30 May 1899 on Wright Cycle Company paper, asking for its papers on flight and a
  reading list ("I am an enthusiast, but not a crank in the sense that I have some pet theories…"); the reply came on 22 June
  with a list (≈3 weeks → «قرابة ثلاث أسابيع»); in May 1900 he wrote to Octave Chanute, who answered four days after receiving
  it with an encouraging letter; they corresponded for years:
  [Smithsonian Archives](https://siarchives.si.edu/node/34346) ·
  [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/collections/wilbur-and-orville-wright-papers/articles-and-essays/the-belief-that-flight-is-possible-to-man/) ·
  [The Henry Ford, the letter](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/artifact/107323).
  First powered flight, 17 December 1903, 12 seconds; the Wright Company, 1909 (check).
- **Photo (door 5):** the first flight, 17 December 1903, taken by John T. Daniels; public domain (Library of Congress),
  via Wikimedia Commons, [File:First flight2.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:First_flight2.jpg) → `first-flight.jpg`
  (960 px). It shows men only (CLAUDE.md rule 7).
- **Evidence:** Flynn (Stanford) and Lake [Bohns], "If You Need Help, Just Ask: Underestimating Compliance With Direct Requests
  for Help", *JPSP*, 2008: people underestimated by as much as 50% how often strangers would agree:
  [Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/if-you-need-help-just-ask-underestimating-compliance-direct-requests) ·
  [Cornell eCommons](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/9c0adbfb-985c-4f7d-aae3-96959822bc4c).
