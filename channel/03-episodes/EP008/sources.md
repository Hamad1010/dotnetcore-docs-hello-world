## Door 1 · George Lucas and the Star Wars rights
- After *American Graffiti* (1973) Lucas could ask Fox for a higher fee; instead of a raise of about $500,000 he took sequel and
  merchandising rights (sources frame it as a waived raise or a $500,000 reduction; one says his salary was $150,000; the deal
  was made before the 1977 release, not dated precisely → «في السبعينات» for the deal, 1973 only for *American Graffiti*):
  [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna7830655) ·
  [Looper](https://looper.com/288092/why-george-lucas-took-a-half-million-dollar-pay-cut-on-the-first-star-wars-movie) ·
  [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/george-lucas-star-wars-rights-gamble) ·
  [SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/2181632/star-wars-business-decision-toy-rights-changed-hollywood/).
  Merchandise: «تُقدّر بأكثر من 20 مليار دولار» is consumer sales over the decades, not Lucas's income (as the sources warn).
  Disney bought Lucasfilm in 2012 for $4.05 billion → «بأكثر من 4 مليار دولار» (check: Disney's press release, 30 Oct 2012).
- **Evidence:** Thompson and Hrebec (Northwestern), "Lose-lose agreements in interdependent decision making", *Psychological
  Bulletin* 120(3), 369–409, 1996: a meta-analysis of 32 experiments; negotiators often fail to see compatible interests and
  settle on outcomes worse for both:
  [Kellogg](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/1996/lose-lose-agreements-in-interdependent-decision-making/?p=1).

## Door 2 · [مثال توضيحي] Salman, an electrician in Madinah
- An illustration; 4,850 / 4,500 / 4,700 are its numbers, labelled on screen.
- **Evidence:** Galinsky (Northwestern) and Mussweiler (Würzburg), "First Offers as Anchors", *JPSP* 81(4), 657–669, 2001: in
  three experiments, whichever side made the first offer got the better outcome, and first offers predicted the final price;
  considering one's alternatives, the opponent's reservation price or one's own target negated the effect:
  [Kellogg](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/research/detail/2001/first-offers-as-anchors-the-role-of-perspective-taking) ·
  [HBS Working Knowledge](https://hbswk.hbs.edu/archive/when-to-make-the-first-offer-in-negotiations).
  Mason, Lee, Wiley and Ames (Columbia), "Precise offers are potent anchors", *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology* 49(4),
  759–763, 2013: precise first offers drew smaller counteroffer adjustments than round ones and seemed better informed:
  [Columbia Business School](https://business.columbia.edu/press-release/cbs-press-releases/new-research-shows-asking-precise-not-round-number-during) ·
  [PON Harvard](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/dealmaking-daily/forming-first-offers-take-precision-account-nb/?amp).
  The 4,850 / 5,000 pair is our example, not the study's numbers.

## Door 3 · Microsoft, IBM and the one clause (1980–1981)
- IBM needed an operating system for its first PC; Microsoft licensed 86-DOS from Seattle Computer Products ($25,000, December
  1980) and bought it outright in July 1981 ($50,000; about $75,000 in all → «بقرابة 75 ألف دولار»); its contract let it
  license the system to other companies; within about a year about 70 companies did:
  [DocuSign, the clause](https://www.docusign.co.uk/blog/clever-clause-made-microsoft/) ·
  [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2011/07/27/ms_dos_turns_30/?page=2) ·
  [History of Information](https://historyofinformation.com/detail.php?entryid=126).
  The IBM PC: August 1981; IBM-compatible clones followed (check).

## Door 4 · [مثال توضيحي] Reem, an events coordinator in Jeddah
- An illustration, labelled on screen; Reem is a watercolor avatar.
- **Evidence:** Langer, Blank and Chanowitz (Harvard), "The mindlessness of ostensibly thoughtful action", *JPSP* 36(6),
  635–642, 1978: at a copier, about 60% let someone cut in with no reason, about 94% with a real reason («لأني مستعجل») and about
  93% with an empty one («لأني أبي أصوّر») for 5 pages; for 20 pages only the real reason helped; later replications are mixed:
  [the study (PDF)](https://jamesclear.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/copy-machine-study-ellen-langer.pdf) ·
  [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/ca/blog/brain-wise/201310/the-power-of-the-word-because-to-get-people-to-do-stuff) ·
  [on the replications](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2024/03/06/mindlessness-in-the-interpretation-of-a-study-on-mindlessness/).

## Door 5 · J. K. Rowling's agent and the split rights
- Rowling taught English in Portugal before returning to Scotland as a single mother on a small income (check); her agent,
  Christopher Little, sold the UK rights of the first Harry Potter book to Bloomsbury for £2,500 (1996), and the US rights were
  auctioned separately and won by Scholastic in 1997 for about $105,000 («بأكثر من 100 ألف دولار»):
  [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2007/07/14/11935611/potter-publisher-predicted-literary-magic) ·
  [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com:443/pw/print/19990215/33543-the-race-for-harry-potter.html) ·
  [Shelf Awareness](https://shelf-awareness.com/theshelf/2021-02-01/obituary_note:_christopher_little.html) ·
  [Leaky Cauldron interview with Arthur Levine](https://www.the-leaky-cauldron.org/?p=8801).
  Not claimed: that Little "kept" the US rights; the script says he sold them separately, at auction.
- **Evidence:** Galinsky and Mussweiler (door 2), experiment 1: thinking of one's alternatives negated the first-offer anchor.
