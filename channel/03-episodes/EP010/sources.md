## Door 1 · Warren Buffett and Benjamin Graham (1951–1954)
- After Graham's class at Columbia, Buffett offered to work at Graham-Newman for nothing (1951); Graham turned him down
  ("overvalued", in Buffett's own telling); Buffett kept sending him securities ideas between 1951 and 1954; in 1954 Graham
  invited him and hired him at $12,000 a year; about two years later (1956) Buffett went home to Omaha and started his own
  partnership, the road to Berkshire Hathaway:
  [GuruFocus, "When I was 25" (Forbes Life)](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/170034/warren-buffett-when-i-was-twentyfive) ·
  [Financial History](https://fhmagazine.org/financial-history-139-fall-2021/0883599001635940960/p27) ·
  [Dividend Growth Investor](https://dividendgrowthinvestor.com/2020/08/warren-buffett-americas-youngest-early.html).
  «كان طالب ماجستير»: Buffett's MS in economics from Columbia, 1951 (check). 1951 → 1954 = 3 → «بعد 3 سنين».
- **Evidence:** Flynn and Lake [Bohns], *JPSP* 2008 (see EP006's sources): people underestimated by as much as 50% how often
  others agree. «لو توقعت إن 2 من 10…»: if the estimate is half the real rate, 2 in 10 becomes 4 in 10, the upper bound
  («ممكن… قريبة من 4»).

## Door 2 · Howard Schultz and Starbucks (1981–1982)
- Selling for Hammarplast (a Swedish housewares maker), Schultz noticed a small Seattle chain ordering many of one drip coffee
  maker, visited, and asked the founders (Jerry Baldwin and Gordon Bowker) to hire him; Baldwin called to say no, fearing he
  would be disruptive; Schultz pleaded with them to reconsider; 24 hours later they offered him the job (director of retail
  operations and marketing); he later bought the company (1987):
  [Encyclopedia.com, citing the Seattle Times](https://encyclopedia.com/reference/trade-magazines/schultz-howard) ·
  [Biography](https://www.biography.com/business-figure/howard-schultz) ·
  [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/business-leaders/starbucks-history).
  «كم فرع بس»: sources give three to five stores at the time.
- **Evidence:** Roghanizad (Waterloo) and Bohns (Cornell), "Ask in person: You're less persuasive than you think over email",
  *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology* 69, 223–226, 2017: 45 participants asked 450 strangers to fill in a short survey
  (half in person, half by email, same script); about 70% agreed in person and about 10% by email; emailers expected email to
  work about as well: [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/?p=129173) · [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2017-05-email-plea.pdf) ·
  [Cornell eCommons](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/e6de77ed-c2f5-4fc3-ab28-68e2e759eff0).
  The study compares face-to-face with email; the script's «بصوتك» claim is only that a spoken ask beats a message, and the
  number is the face-to-face one.

## Door 3 · Chicken Soup for the Soul
- Jack Canfield and Mark Victor Hansen's book was turned down by 144 publishers (their own count; some sources say 140 or
  "more than 130"); Health Communications published it in 1993; it reached the bestseller lists about 16 months later (around
  September 1994); the series has more than 200 books:
  [Chicken Soup for the Soul](https://www.chickensoup.com/press-release/chicken-soup-for-the-soul-30th-anniversary-edition) ·
  [Literary Hub](https://lithub.com/the-most-rejected-books-of-all-time/) ·
  [HowStuffWorks](https://entertainment.howstuffworks.com/arts/literature/14-best-selling-books-repeatedly-rejected-by-publishers.htm) ·
  [Indy Tale](https://issuu.com/indtalemagazine/docs/may_2023_mag/s/23553202).
  Left out (disputed): the deal terms and total sales.

## Door 4 · Jia Jiang's 100 days of rejection (2012)
- Day 1: he asked a stranger to lend him $100, was asked why, and ran (his TED talk); day 3: at a Krispy Kreme in Austin,
  Texas, Jackie Braun made him doughnuts in the shape of the Olympic rings and did not charge him; the video spread widely; his
  talk "What I Learned From 100 Days of Rejection" (over ten million views, per one source) and the book *Rejection Proof*:
  [TIME](https://newsfeed.time.com/?p=184650) · [Yahoo News](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/blogs/sideshow/krispy-kreme-donut-order-rejection-video-153858341.html) ·
  [TED transcript](https://singjupost.com/jia-jiang-what-i-learned-from-100-days-of-rejection-at-ted-talk-transcript/) ·
  [Penguin Random House](https://penguinrandomhouse.com/books/239453/rejection-proof-by-jia-jiang).
  Jackie is a watercolor avatar.
- **Evidence:** Flynn and Lake 2008 (door 1).

## Door 5 · Soichiro Honda's piston rings
- In the 1930s Honda's Tokai Seiki made piston rings for the young Toyota Motor Company; of the first fifty, only three passed
  inspection (one source); he studied and improved the process (he attended technical school to learn metallurgy: check);
  about two years later Toyota placed a large order; after the war he sold what was left to Toyota for ¥450,000 and founded the
  Honda Technical Research Institute (October 1946), then Honda Motor (September 1948):
  [Association for Asian Studies](https://www.asianstudies.org/eaa/honda-soichiro-and-the-rise-of-japans-postwar-motor-vehicle-industry/) ·
  [ASME](https://www.asme.org/topics-resources/content/soichiro-honda) ·
  [Honda heritage](https://global.honda/en/heritage/episodes/1948doyouremember.html).
  3 ÷ 50 = 6% → «قرابة 6%»; 47 rejected → «94% رفض».
